# Raja Ampat Liveaboard: How to Choose Your Boat

A Raja Ampat liveaboard is the way to reach the far reefs of the archipelago, and most trips run 7 to 12 days from Sorong or Waisai between October and April. Pick the route first (the Dampier Strait in the centre, or Misool in the south), then the boat, then whether you dive or snorkel.

Raja Ampat, off the western tip of New Guinea in Southwest Papua, has the richest coral and reef fish life recorded anywhere. It is also remote: two flights and a ferry from most of the world. A land base on Kri or Waigeo shows you the nearby reefs at an easy pace. A liveaboard moves on every night, and it is the only practical way to dive several regions in one trip.

## How long is a typical Raja Ampat liveaboard trip?

Seven days and six nights is the common minimum, and it usually stays in the central area around the Dampier Strait: Kri, Mansuar, Arborek, the Fam Islands and the Piaynemo viewpoint. Nine to twelve days adds Misool in the south, which means an overnight crossing each way and some of the region's best soft coral walls.

Add a day of travel at each end. You fly to Sorong (airport code SOQ), usually through Makassar or Jakarta, and boats either board in Sorong or in Waisai on Waigeo, about two hours away on the Express Bahari ferry. Book flights that leave Sorong in the afternoon of your last day, since most trips are back around midday.

## Raja Ampat diving liveaboard or snorkeling liveaboard?

Both work here, because so much of the reef is shallow. Snorkelling off a village jetty in Raja Ampat beats dedicated dive sites in many countries. The difference is in the boat and the day.

| | Diving liveaboard | Snorkeling liveaboard |
|---|---|---|
| Typical day | Three or four guided dives, sometimes a night dive | Several snorkel stops, beaches, viewpoints, villages |
| Who it suits | Certified divers, ideally with some current experience | Non-divers, families, mixed groups |
| Cabins | From shared bunks to private doubles | Often simpler, shared berths and bathrooms |
| Extras | Rental gear and dive insurance | Check whether snorkel gear is supplied |
| Can the other half join? | Non-divers come along but miss the main activity | Some boats let certified divers add dives on one day |

The Dampier Strait has strong currents at several famous sites, and that is part of why the fish life is so dense. If you have not dived in current before, choose a boat that talks about matching sites to guest experience.

## When is the best time for a Raja Ampat liveaboard?

October to April is the season. Mid-October to mid-December has the calmest seas and clearest water, and manta encounters in the Dampier Strait are most reliable from about October to May. Raja Ampat sits almost on the equator, so rain comes in short bursts all year: wind, not rain, is what decides your trip.

From mid-June to mid-September the south-east trade winds blow at 20 to 30 knots for weeks at a time, which makes the Dampier crossings and the open water to Misool unpleasant. That is when most liveaboards sail west to Komodo, whose season runs the other way round. If June to September is your only window, the [Labuan Bajo and Komodo guide](labuan-bajo-komodo-guide.md) is where the fleet goes.

## How much does a Raja Ampat liveaboard trip cost?

Fares vary more by cabin than by boat: a shared berth with a shared bathroom costs far less than a private double with its own. All fares on the boats below are per person for the whole trip, with meals on board included.

What the boat fares leave out is the official fees. Every foreign visitor now pays two of them: the marine park permit, valid for 12 months from purchase, and a separate visitor entry ticket charged per visit. The permit price rose in August 2026, so older guides understate it. Some boats, Epica among them, add a harbour fee per guest, and divers pay for dive insurance and any rental gear on top.

Cash is the other trap. The only ATMs are in Waisai, they often refuse foreign cards, and there is no cash at all out in the islands. Draw what you need before you land in Sorong.

## How to book a Raja Ampat liveaboard

1. **Fix your dates against the wind.** October to April, and earlier in the season for calmer crossings.
2. **Choose the route.** Seven days for the Dampier Strait, nine or more if you want Misool.
3. **Choose the cabin.** Decide whether a shared bathroom is acceptable, since it changes the price more than anything else.
4. **Check the departure.** Most boats run scheduled departures and only listed dates have a confirmed fare. Ask for space before paying.
5. **Read the cancellation terms.** Liveaboards generally take a deposit and the balance well before departure, so check what is refundable, and until when, before you pay.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which is better for a liveaboard, Raja Ampat or Komodo?

They suit different months, so the calendar often decides. Raja Ampat has the richer reef and the bigger range of coral and fish; Komodo is easier to reach from Bali and adds the dragons and Padar on land. Our [Indonesia tours guide](indonesia-tours.md) compares both, and the [Southeast Asia travel guide](README.md) covers the rest of the region.

### Can non-divers join a Raja Ampat liveaboard?

Yes. Snorkelling is excellent across most of the archipelago, and dedicated snorkel liveaboards plan every stop around it, with beaches and viewpoints in between. On a dive boat a non-diver can come along, but the day is built around three or four dives, so a snorkel boat is the better fit.

### Is a Raja Ampat liveaboard suitable for beginner divers?

It can be, with care. Some sites in the Dampier Strait have strong currents, so new divers should pick a boat that chooses sites for guest experience and runs a check dive on day one. Logging dives in current before the trip, even a handful, makes the week far more enjoyable.

### Do I need to buy the Raja Ampat permit before I arrive?

Register online before you fly: the authority now asks for registration through its own portal, and your proof of payment is personal and gets checked. Some boats collect the fees on board and some ask you to repay them in Sorong, so ask which applies before departure.

Settle your dates against the October to April window first, then ask for space on two or three boats at once.
